# Regulation Compliance – DPDP Act 2023

## Objective
To study the requirements of the Digital Personal Data Protection
(DPDP) Act, 2023 and develop a plan for ensuring compliance of the
University of Delhi Student Portal.

## 1. About the Regulation
The DPDP Act, 2023 is India's data protection law. It applies to
digital personal data processed in India.

Key terms:
- Data Principal: the person whose data it is (the student)
- Data Fiduciary: the entity deciding why and how data is processed (the university)
- Data Protection Board of India: the body that handles complaints and penalties

## 2. Principles of the Act
- Lawful purpose and consent
- Notice (tell the person what is collected and why)
- Purpose limitation and data minimization
- Accuracy
- Storage limitation (erase when purpose is served)
- Security safeguards
- Breach notification
- Accountability

## 3. Compliance Mapping

| Requirement | DPDP Section | What the portal should do | Status | Gap / Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notice | Sec 5 | Show what data is collected and why, before collection | Partial | Add a clear privacy notice on the login/registration page |
| Consent | Sec 6 | Free, specific, informed, withdrawable consent | Partial | Add consent record and a withdrawal option |
| Purpose limitation | Sec 4 | Use data only for academic/admin purposes | Compliant | Document purposes formally |
| Data minimization | Sec 4, 6 | Collect only necessary fields | Partial | Review which fields are really needed |
| Accuracy | Sec 8 | Keep student data correct and updated | Partial | Give students a way to request corrections |
| Security safeguards | Sec 8 | Encryption, access control, logging | Partial | Add 2FA, encrypt uploaded documents, keep audit logs |
| Breach notification | Sec 8 | Inform the Board and affected students | Not found | Create a breach response procedure |
| Retention / erasure | Sec 8 | Delete data once purpose is served | Not found | Define retention periods for alumni data |
| Children's data | Sec 9 | Parental consent for under-18 users | Not verified | Add age check for students under 18 |
| Data Principal rights | Sec 11-14 | Access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal | Partial | Publish a grievance officer contact |

## 4. Penalties for Non-Compliance
The Act allows penalties of up to Rs 250 crore for failure to
maintain reasonable security safeguards, and up to Rs 200 crore for
failure to notify a breach (Sec 33 and Schedule).

## 5. Compliance Plan

Step 1: Data inventory – list all personal data the portal holds.
Step 2: Define purpose for each data type.
Step 3: Add privacy notice and consent mechanism.
Step 4: Strengthen security (2FA, encryption, role-based access, logs).
Step 5: Set retention rules and deletion process.
Step 6: Build a rights-request process (access, correct, erase).
Step 7: Appoint a grievance officer and publish contact details.
Step 8: Prepare a breach response plan (links to Practical 8).
Step 9: Train staff and review compliance yearly.

## 6. Conclusion
Mapping the portal against the DPDP Act shows gaps mainly in notice,
consent management, retention and breach handling. Following the
compliance plan would align the portal with the Act and protect
students' privacy rights.
